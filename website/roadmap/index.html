    BTC / XMZ

    Second pair · public test-network swap passed
    Tests and remaining work

    How a BTC/XMZ swap works, step by step

    New (28 Oct): two more runs passed with the seller using only light servers. Silent buyer: the buyer locked its Bitcoin and then stopped responding; the seller cancelled the trade after the timelock, waited the agreed punishment period and claimed the Bitcoin (0.9998 test BTC), all checked through the servers (about 50 minutes). The first attempts at this were refused by the app: its check treated the cancellation, which pays back into the same locked script, as if the coins were already spent. That rule was too strict, never too loose; it now counts only a transaction that actually spends the coins. Dishonest server: during a complete swap one of the two servers hid the newest block when the seller tried to redeem, and later hid the confirmed redemption. The app refused both times and nothing was sent; once the server answered honestly the swap finished with exact amounts (about 16 minutes). Isolated test chains, test coins only.

    New (26 Oct): the XMZ seller no longer needs its own Bitcoin node. Its app can now check the buyer’s Bitcoin lock, redeem it and confirm the redemption through two or more independent Electrum servers instead. Inclusion and confirmations are proven against block headers the app validates itself. What headers cannot prove (that the lock is still unspent, and that no newer blocks are being withheld) must be agreed by every server, the newest block must be recent, and any disagreement stops the trade before anything is released. With the seller using only two such servers, three runs passed: a complete swap on isolated test chains (about 15 minutes); a refund there, where after the timelock the buyer’s Bitcoin returned to its light wallet and the seller recovered its XMZ by reading the buyer’s refund through the servers (about 44 minutes); and a complete swap on public Bitcoin signet, where the seller redeemed 0.0019 signet BTC and the buyer received exactly 1 XMZ (about 49 minutes). The first attempts stopped safely before release and exposed two app settings that were then fixed. In these tests both servers read our own test node, so they check the rules and the app, not real server independence. Test coins only.

    Update (25 Oct, evening): four more signet runs passed. Refund: after the timelock, the buyer’s 0.0018 signet BTC returned to the app’s light wallet and the seller’s XMZ was refunded (about 4 hours 24 minutes). Two machines: the buyer ran on a separate machine whose app created its own identity, light wallet and XMZ wallets; the other machine only saw its public card. The swap completed with exact amounts (about 2 hours). Interrupted: the buyer’s app was stopped right after signing its Bitcoin lock, reopened from its saved files, sent the same signed transaction and completed the swap (about 1 hour 12 minutes). Power loss: the buyer’s machine was switched off without shutting down right after signing; after it rebooted, its saved wallet and trade files were intact, it sent the same signed transaction and the swap completed with exact amounts (about 1 hour 39 minutes). Separately, the light wallet is ready for Bitcoin mainnet headers: all 973,097 real mainnet headers validate from genesis, and wallets start from a checkpoint pinned from our own node. Mainnet trading stays disabled.

    New (25 Oct): the first complete BTC/XMZ swap on a public Bitcoin test network passed, on Bitcoin signet. The app’s light wallet locked 0.002 signet BTC; the seller locked XMZ on the Monzero test network; the seller redeemed 0.0019 signet BTC and the buyer received exactly 1 XMZ, with the seller’s reserve returned. Every step went through the app, and each confirmation was checked by the app itself (about 1 hour 45 minutes). The light wallet now follows signet rules: all 327,823 real signet headers validate from genesis, and wallets start from a checkpoint pinned from our own node. Signet blocks are signed by the network operators, which the light wallet cannot check from headers alone, so it relies on our own node for that. Test coins only.

    Testnet4 refund (23–25 Oct): the first testnet4 trade locked 0.002 test BTC, then the test harness stopped before XMZ was locked. A restarted app reopened the saved trade and cancelled it after the 36-block timelock. Testnet4 then mined only empty blocks for about two days, so the cancellation waited in the mempool. Once it confirmed, the reopened app refunded 0.0018 test BTC to the light wallet (0.0002 went on the two network fees). Recovery after a restart therefore works on a public network too.

    20 Oct: BTC/XMZ now runs on the same contract, recovery and light-wallet path as LTC/XMZ, which Bitcoin can share because both use the same SegWit scripts and timelocks. A Bitcoin light wallet inside the app takes deposits at its own address, funds the Bitcoin lock and receives any refund. On isolated chains, with both producing blocks on their own clock, a complete swap passed: Bitcoin lock from the light wallet, XMZ lock, Bitcoin redemption and XMZ payout, with exact amounts and fees (about 18 minutes). A refund also passed: after the timelock, 0.9998 test BTC returned to the light wallet and the XMZ was refunded (about 42 minutes). The light wallet now follows Bitcoin testnet4 rules; all 157,145 real testnet4 headers validate from genesis, and a new wallet synced to the live testnet4 tip in 2 seconds. Engine, 82 native Qt and 165 QML checks pass. A first attempt stopped safely when a block arrived between funding review and signing: nothing was signed or sent, and fresh approval is required.

    The 19 September checkpoint records isolated funded settlement and lost-broadcast-reply handling without duplicate payment.

    That checkpoint reports 89 engine library tests and three compile-fail checks; wallet approval checks passed with GCC and Clang.

    New (13 Oct): the shared light wallet now supports Bitcoin (native SegWit). Regtest tests against Bitcoin Core 31.1 pass for receiving, sending and confirmation, both through the helper and through the app’s Qt worker.

    Still to verify: The XMZ seller’s light-server mode on a public test network and an outside review of its rules, mainnet support, release validation and activation. Mainnet trading remains disabled in the code.

    ETH · BNB / XMZ

    BNB mainnet test
    Tests and remaining work

    One swap contract serves BNB Chain and Ethereum. Each trader keeps their own funds until the swap completes or refunds; there is no custodian, bridge or wrapped token.

    On a local EVM chain with a local XMZ chain and real XMZ wallets, a full XMZ↔BNB swap and a refunded swap both pass. Traders exchange keys with a signed, cross-curve-proven frame, and stuck transactions are re-sent with capped higher fees.

    30 October: the swap contract is live on BNB Smart Chain testnet and the first public-testnet XMZ↔BNB swap completed. Each side locked its own coins, the XMZ seller claimed the tBNB and the buyer swept the XMZ to their own wallet. Traders exchanged keys over the peer-to-peer network, and their keys and BNB account come from the wallet's own recovery seed. The run also found and fixed connection handling for public BNB endpoints.

    The refund path also passed on BNB testnet the same day: the XMZ seller went offline after the buyer marked the swap ready, the buyer refunded their tBNB after the deadline, and the seller then used the revealed key to take their XMZ back. Each side lost only network fees. Trades are now saved in their journal before anything is locked, so a restarted app rebuilds them from the wallet seed, and no coins are locked until the user approves that exact trade. In a further BNB testnet swap both traders were force-stopped 22 times, at every stage and at random moments; the swap still completed and every transaction was sent exactly once. Later the same day a swap ran between two machines, and the XMZ seller's machine had its power cut twice, right after locking its XMZ and right after claiming; it restarted, carried on and finished. The power cuts also found, and led to a fix for, a wallet file left empty by a power loss. That evening the Monzero app itself completed a BNB testnet trade: it showed the amounts, waited for the user's approval, then locked the BNB, confirmed the XMZ and moved the XMZ into its own wallet without further clicks.

    31 October: the same swap contract was deployed on BNB Smart Chain mainnet (0x9073290952DCDF3225D8f65d787cF9148a490feb, identical code to the testnet contract, with no owner or admin) and the first atomic swap with real BNB and real XMZ completed. It was the project's own controlled test between two dedicated test accounts: the buyer locked BNB, the seller locked XMZ, the seller collected the BNB and the buyer swept the XMZ into their own wallet. Both sides finished without errors, nobody held anyone else's coins, and the contract holds nothing afterwards. The wallet's vault can now also derive the BNB swap keys itself, so the recovery words never leave it.

    Still to verify: The refund path with real funds, finding trading partners inside the app, the 1% project fee inside the swap, and community security testing against a published test plan.

    Light-wallet swap on the public Litecoin testnet

    No Litecoin node needed: on 17 October the app's own Litecoin light wallet paid the Litecoin side of an LTC/XMZ swap on the public Litecoin testnet, with no Litecoin node wallet involved. The app reviewed the payment, signed it without sending, sent it only after a separate approval, re-sent the identical transaction, and then saw it confirmed in a block through the light wallet. The XMZ lock, Litecoin redemption and XMZ payout followed, and every app check passed on its first attempt. Afterwards the light wallet held exactly its change: 0.25 tLTC funded, minus the 0.2 tLTC traded, minus a 0.00001 tLTC fee. The whole swap took 94 minutes.

    Problem found and fixed: the app only offered light-wallet funding when the wallet was on a local test chain, so on testnet the option never appeared. It now follows the network chosen at startup.

    Dropped connections now recover by themselves: if the connection between the two traders drops in the middle of a trade, the apps now reconnect on their own instead of waiting for a restart. They rebuild the trade session from what is saved on disk, the same steps an app restart takes, and retry at growing intervals. They also re-exchange the already signed refund agreement, so the trade carries on. Messages that fail a check still stop the trade, and nothing is retried in that case. If the drop happens during the final settlement messages, the apps repeat only the steps already under way before the drop: the funding proof, and a release the user had already approved for that same payment. No new approval is ever created, and a settlement that had finished stays finished. In tests the connection was cut on each side after both coins were locked, after the funding proof, and after the release, and once more during a refund. The apps reconnected within 33–54 seconds, and all six trades finished with every check passing on its first try.

    Litecoin MWEB payments: Litecoin's private MWEB layer stores some transactions in an extended format that the light wallet could not read. So a payment out of an MWEB wallet into a light wallet, a common way to send Litecoin, would have stopped the light wallet from refreshing. The light wallet now reads these transactions while still checking every one against its block. On the public Litecoin testnet, 0.02 tLTC was sent from an MWEB-only wallet to a light wallet: the previous version failed to refresh, and the fixed version showed exactly 0.02 tLTC more.

    Fast light-wallet startup: the light wallet used to check every Litecoin block header from the start of the chain each time it started, about 19 minutes on testnet. It now starts from a built-in checkpoint and, on restart, rechecks proof of work only for the most recent headers, while still checking that every saved header links to the one before it. A testnet sync now takes under a second and stores 230 KB instead of 394 MB. Mainnet still needs its own checkpoint.

    New · 16 October

    Monzero Pool is online

    Mine XMZ together: the project now runs a public RandomX mining pool on the same server as the public node. Point XMRig at pool.monzero.org:3333 (CPUs and laptops) or :5555 (servers), with your own XMZ address as the username: xmrig -o pool.monzero.org:3333 -u YOUR_XMZ_ADDRESS -p rig1 -a rx/0. Live statistics are at pool.monzero.org.

    How it pays: PPLNS with a 1% fee. Blocks are credited after 60 confirmations, and only if the chain still holds the exact block the pool found. Payouts are on: balances of 1 XMZ or more are paid every hour. The pool's first 14 blocks were credited to the atomic unit before payments were switched on.

    Built for this chain: the pool was written for Monzero and checks every share with the node's own proof-of-work code. Tests on a private chain cover rejected shares, found blocks, orphaned blocks, exact reward splits and payments arriving in miners' wallets. A pool also lets Monzero be listed on mining statistics sites, and more independent miners make the network harder to attack.

    DURABLE RECOVERY

    Close the app. Keep the trade safe.

    BTC/XMZ passed two-machine, interrupted-app and hard power-off runs on Bitcoin signet (25 October), and LTC/XMZ passed a real power-loss run (18 October). Light-wallet trades recover from a killed app or helper and from a lost broadcast reply without duplicate funding, and each trade has its own folder that the app reopens by itself. On the XMR route, a maker restarted after the cancel safely aborted without sending XMR. XMZ↔BNB swaps resume from their saved journal: a BNB testnet swap force-stopped 22 times still completed with each transaction sent once, and a two-machine swap survived two real power cuts. Remaining: the same runs for the joined XMR route.

    Deliverables & completion gate
    • Persist signed terms, peer identities, policy and protocol progress.
    • Connect single-use execution ownership to a restart-safe journal.
    • Test crashes, interrupted writes, missing replies and uncertain broadcasts.
    • Reconcile an existing payment instead of blindly sending it again.
    Exit condition

    A fresh application process resumes or safely reconciles interrupted swaps without duplicate funding or loss of recovery information.

    Builds on: authenticated Litecoin state, prepared XMZ transactions and isolated recovery tests

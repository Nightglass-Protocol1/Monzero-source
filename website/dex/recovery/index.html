Monzero DEX · self-custody

Recover your swap from zero.

A Monzero atomic swap is non-custodial: no one — not Monzero, not the trader on the other side — can move your coins. That also means recovery is in your hands. This guide covers every market on the DEX today — XMZ against ETH, BNB, SOL and USDT (on Solana and on BNB Chain) — from the everyday fixes to the rare worst case.

Read first

A swap is short-lived: once coins are locked it completes or refunds within about four hours (section 6). The wallet does every protective step by itself — claims, refunds and sweeps never wait for a click. Your only job is to keep the wallet able to come back: back up its trading data and password (section 1), and reopen it if it closes mid-trade.

1Back up your trading data

Trading uses a separate set of trading keys, made when you first chose a trading password. They control your trade account (where ETH, BNB, SOL or USDT is paid in and paid out) and the small in-app XMZ trading wallet. Each trade's own keys are derived from them, and each trade keeps a journal of where it is.

WalletWhat to keep safe
Linux desktopThe folder ~/.local/share/monzero-project/monzero-core/dex/ and your trading password
Windows desktop (beta)The folder %APPDATA%\monzero-project\monzero-core\dex\ and your trading password
AndroidThe twelve trading recovery words the app showed when it made your trading keys, and your trading password. Keep the phone's wallet app installed while a trade runs: its trade journals live inside the app.

On the desktop the trading keys are stored encrypted inside that folder — nobody can restore the password, and there is no separate word list, so the folder plus the password is the backup. Inside it, trading-keys/ holds the keys, evm-trades/ one folder per offer or trade (each running trade has a journal.json), and xmz-wallet/ the trading XMZ wallet. Copy the whole dex folder (with the wallet closed) to somewhere private (an encrypted USB stick, for example), and do it again while a trade is running. Treat it like a seed backup.

2The quick fix: reopen the wallet

If the wallet closed, crashed, or lost power mid-swap, just open it again and unlock trading with your password. It resumes every unfinished trade from its journal, reads both chains, and takes the right path by itself: claim, refund, or sweep the XMZ.

  • Desktop: open the wallet, then Exchange, and enter your trading password. Running trades show under My orders.
  • Android: open the wallet app; it asks for the trading password in a notification, then carries on. While a trade runs the app keeps itself awake — let it.
  • Web page: monzero.org/dex only talks to the wallet on your device. Closing the browser changes nothing; the wallet runs the trade. Reload the page and connect again to watch it.

Leave the wallet running until the trade says swapped or refunded. If you are the XMZ seller, it must be online at some point before the claim deadline (section 6) to collect your coin — and even if it misses that, your XMZ comes back (section 8).

3Coin left in your trade account

ETH, BNB, SOL and USDT go through your wallet's own trade account — paid in before a trade, paid out after it. When a trade started from the web page finishes, the wallet sends the account's coin back to the MetaMask or Phantom account you connected, by itself. Coin can still be left there — an offer you cancelled, one nobody took, a take the maker refused, or proceeds from a trade made in the desktop Exchange. To move it:

  • Desktop wallet: Exchange → pick the coin → Withdraw to your own … wallet, paste any address of yours on that chain, and press Withdraw.
  • Web page: connect, then Send it back to MetaMask / Phantom. It only ever pays the account you connected.
If Withdraw refuses

The wallet will not empty a trade account that something still needs. It says why: "an offer of yours is still open: cancel it first" or "a trade is still running". Cancel your open offers on that coin (section 4), or let the running trade finish or refund, then withdraw again. SOL and USDT on Solana share one trade account, so an open offer on either market holds both.

4An order or take that won't go away

Nothing is locked on any chain until you approve a trade in the wallet. Before that, an offer or a take is only a message, and can always be dropped:

  • Your open offer: press Cancel next to it (desktop: Exchange → My orders; web page: Your open offers). It leaves the order book and stops holding your trade account. If you do nothing, it ends by itself when its time runs out.
  • A take waiting for the maker: if the other trader never answers — for example, they withdrew the offer — press Stop waiting on the web page (Waiting for the maker). Otherwise it ends by itself when the offer expires. Nothing of yours was locked.
  • A trade you approved: this one can't be cancelled — it runs to swapped or refunded on its own (section 6). Keep the wallet open or reopen it.

5Lost the device, kept the data

  1. Desktop: install the Monzero wallet on any computer, put your backed-up dex folder back in the location from section 1 (with the wallet closed), open the wallet and unlock trading with the same password. If it says your trading keys were set up by another copy of the wallet, accept the switch. Unfinished trades resume from their journals.
  2. Android: your twelve trading recovery words open the trade account in MetaMask or any Ethereum wallet: it is the standard account m/44'/60'/0'/0/0 of those words, so ETH, BNB and USDT on BNB Chain left in it are yours to move. Restoring the words into a new phone's wallet app is not available yet; if the lost phone had a trade running, or SOL or USDT on Solana in its trade account, email us (section 7).

Nothing secret ever left your device, so a fresh install plus your backup is a complete recovery. A trade can only be rebuilt with the same keys it started with; a different backup is refused rather than producing a different trade.

6Timeout reference

Every trade has two deadlines, set on chain when the buyer of XMZ locks their coin in the swap contract (the Solana program on Solana) — timeout 1 two hours after the lock, timeout 2 two hours after that. The same rules apply on Ethereum, BNB Chain and Solana, for the native coin and for USDT:

WhenWhoWhat happens
Right after agreeingbuyer of XMZlocks ETH/BNB/SOL/USDT in the contract (the seller waits about 30 min, 1 h on Ethereum, then gives up — nothing lost)
Before timeout 1seller of XMZlocks the XMZ in a key shared by both sides
Before timeout 1buyerconfirms the XMZ lock (ready) — or, if no correct XMZ arrived, takes the coin straight back (early refund)
From ready (or timeout 1) until timeout 2sellerclaims the coin; the claim reveals what the buyer needs, and the buyer's wallet sweeps the XMZ
From timeout 2buyerif the seller never claimed: refunds the coin; the refund reveals what the seller needs, and the seller's wallet sweeps the XMZ back

So a trade is settled within about four hours of the lock, either way. The wallet keeps safety margins: it never claims in the last 30 minutes before timeout 2, and never confirms ready late.

7No Monzero app at all (advanced)

Even without Monzero software, the outcome is recoverable, because the contracts and the trade format are open source (Monzero-source, under gui/dex-contracts):

  • Coin side: the swap contract has no owner, admin key or pause. Its refund and claim calls can be made from any Ethereum, BNB Chain or Solana tool with your trade account's key, once their time has come (section 6).
  • XMZ side: the XMZ sits in a key shared by both sides. Your share is derived from your trading keys and the offer; the other half is revealed on chain by the claim or the refund.
Honest limitation

This last-resort path is real but not a one-click, non-technical procedure. If you are ever in this situation, email security@monzero.org and we will walk you through it. We can help you recover your funds; we cannot take them — there is no key on our side that can.

8What can and can't happen to your funds

  • Your keys never leave your device. Monzero holds no copy and has no admin key. We cannot freeze, move, or seize a swap — in flight or settled. The web page can't either: it can ask, but only the wallet signs, and only after you approve the trade there.
  • The blockchains enforce the outcome. Either both sides get paid, or both get their coins back. There is no third outcome where one side keeps everything.
  • The wallet's job is timing, not custody. A seller that misses the claim deadline does not lose the XMZ — the buyer's refund lets the wallet sweep it back. The relays and the website can delay a trade at worst, never redirect it.
  • The real risk is losing your trading keys — the backup and password from section 1 — while coins are in your trade account or a trade is running.

Questions, or stuck mid-swap? security@monzero.org, or the Monzero Telegram. The source for everything above is open: Monzero-source.

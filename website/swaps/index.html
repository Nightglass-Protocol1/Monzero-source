Monzero DEX · atomic swap

How a BTC / XMZ swap works

Two strangers trade Bitcoin for Monzero without an exchange, an escrow agent or trusting each other. The two blockchains enforce every step: either both sides get paid, or both get their coins back.

Test networks only. These swaps run on Bitcoin signet and the Monzero test network. Mainnet trading is switched off in the code.

The two traders

Bob · buying XMZ

Pays with Bitcoin

Uses the Bitcoin light wallet built into the Monzero app, so he doesn't need to run a Bitcoin node.

Alice · selling XMZ

Gets paid in Bitcoin

Uses her Monzero wallet. For now her app also needs a Bitcoin node to check the chain; a light-wallet option for sellers is being designed.

Step by step

Amounts are from the signet test swap on 25 October: 0.002 BTC for 1 XMZ, with a 36-block safety timelock.

Bob · BTCOn-chainAlice · XMZ
STEP 1 · AGREE

Both apps sign the deal before any money moves

Bob accepts Alice's signed quote. The apps exchange identities, keys and proofs, then each saves pre-signed cancel and refund transactions. From here, either side can always get their coins back.

STEP 2 · BOB LOCKS BTC

0.002 BTC goes into a shared contract

Bob approves the payment in his app. The coins sit in a 2-of-2 contract: spending them needs both traders' keys, or the timelock to pass.

→Bitcoinlock confirms
Monzero←10 confirmations
STEP 3 · ALICE LOCKS XMZ

1 XMZ goes to a shared address

Alice's app checks Bob's lock first. Then she sends 1 XMZ plus a small fee reserve to an address that can only be spent with both traders' key shares.

STEP 4 · BOB HANDS OVER A LOCKED SIGNATURE

A signature with a catch

Bob checks the XMZ lock, then gives Alice an adaptor signature for claiming the BTC. To use it, Alice has to reveal her XMZ key share.

→sent app to app
Bitcoin←redeem published
STEP 5 · ALICE CLAIMS THE BTC

0.0019 BTC to Alice

While the timelock is still safely far away, Alice completes the signature and broadcasts it. 0.0001 BTC goes on the network fee. Publishing it puts her XMZ key share on the Bitcoin chain.

STEP 6 · BOB CLAIMS THE XMZ

Exactly 1 XMZ to Bob

Bob's app reads Alice's redeem from the Bitcoin chain, recovers her key share and sweeps the 1 XMZ to his wallet. The unused fee reserve goes back to Alice.

←swap done

Why neither side can cheat

Alice can only take the Bitcoin by publishing the very thing Bob needs to take the XMZ. If she never claims, the timelock runs out and Bob gets his Bitcoin back.

Bob · BTCOn-chainAlice · XMZ
THE TRIGGER

Alice never locks her XMZ, or never claims before the timelock

Bob's 0.002 BTC is sitting in the contract. Nobody can take it alone, and the 36-block timelock is counting down.

AFTER 36 BLOCKS · CANCEL

Bob broadcasts the pre-signed cancel

This moves the coins out of the swap contract into a refund contract. It was signed by both apps back in step 1, so Alice's help isn't needed.

→Bitcoincancel confirms
REFUND

0.0018 BTC back in Bob's light wallet

Bob's app sends the refund. The 0.0002 BTC difference is the two network fees. Tested on testnet4 and on signet.

→BTC refunded
Monzero←XMZ refunded
ALICE'S XMZ

Bob's refund unlocks Alice's XMZ

If Alice did lock XMZ, Bob's refund transaction reveals his key share, so Alice's app can sweep her XMZ back.

Bitcoin←punish
IF BOB VANISHES AFTER CANCEL

Alice can take the BTC

If Bob cancels but never refunds, after 10 more blocks Alice can punish and take the Bitcoin. Otherwise her XMZ would stay stuck, so this keeps both sides honest.

Alice's app refuses to claim when the timelock is close; it keeps a safety margin of several blocks. On Bitcoin, 36 blocks is about six hours.

What if an app crashes mid-trade?

Everything is saved before it is sent

Each signed transaction is written to disk before broadcast. A reopened app sends the same bytes again: no double payment, and nothing is signed twice.

Tested with the power pulled

On 25 October the buyer's machine was switched off mid-swap without shutting down. After reboot its wallet and trade files were intact and the swap finished with exact amounts.

Approvals don't carry over

After a restart, any approval that hadn't been used is cleared. You review and approve again with fresh chain data.

Each app checks the chain itself

Neither app takes the other trader's word for a payment. Bob's light wallet verifies Bitcoin block headers and proofs; Alice's app checks with her own node.
